In plain terms

  • About 84% of reserves sit in one government money market fund, registered with the SEC, run by BlackRock and held in custody at BNY. The rest is cash at banks.
  • The fund holds short Treasury bills and overnight repo (overnight loans of cash backed by Treasuries). Its average holding matures in about two weeks.
  • Circle publishes weekly reserve figures, and a Big Four accounting firm gives a monthly attestation that reserves exceed USDC outstanding.
  • A dollar split of the cash held at banks, by bank name: not available in Circle's public figures.

Why it matters to a community bank

  • The GENIUS Act bars stablecoin issuers from paying interest. ABA and ICBA want that ban extended to exchanges and affiliates.
  • Compare section 3: average savings pays 0.38%, while a Coinbase One member can earn up to 3.75% in USDC "rewards".

What a dollar earns: deposits vs. Treasuries vs. USDC

FDIC national averages are monthly (as of Oct 19, 2026). Fed funds (NY Fed) and the Treasury bill rate (Treasury) refresh live when the page opens. APY = annual percentage yield.

Rates side by side (%)

How to read this: Circle earns roughly Treasury bill rates on USDC reserves; USDC holders themselves get no interest from Circle. Holders earn only if a third party pays "rewards" (like Coinbase) or if they lend USDC out (see "What USDC lending earns" below), which carries credit and technology risk and no FDIC insurance. The gap between these rates and average savings rates is the heart of the deposit competition debate (see "The deposit competition debate" above).

What USDC lending earns

"DeFi lending" (decentralized finance) means automated lending pools run by software on a blockchain: lenders deposit USDC, borrowers post crypto collateral, and rates float with demand. No bank stands in the middle.

The "rewards" question

The GENIUS Act bars stablecoin issuers from paying interest. On Oct 21, 2026, federal bank regulators said crypto firms can keep paying rewards that work like interest, and the OCC will review arrangements with third parties case by case. Bank groups (ABA, ICBA, state associations including CBAO) want the ban widened. AP, Oct 21, 2026

Oklahoma: HB 3521 and the state banking regulator

Oklahoma HB 3521: Money Transmission Modernization Act

  • Takes effect November 1, 2026 (Section 56). New law at 6 O.S. §1551 and following.
  • Stablecoins count as "money." The definition of money includes a stablecoin that is pegged to a government currency, fully backed by reserves, and redeemable from the issuer (Section 3, ¶21). USDC is designed to fit that description.
  • Banks are outside the Act. It does not apply to a federally insured depository institution or bank holding company (Section 4, ¶7). It mainly governs money transmitters that are not banks.
  • Oklahoma's state banking regulator has said HB 3521 replaces its current money transmitter rules (OAC 85:15). As of Oct 22, 2026 we found no published implementing rules.
Sources: Enrolled HB 3521 text, bill page, state banking regulator bulletin. Read Oct 22, 2026. A summary, not legal advice; ask counsel how it applies to your bank.

4. Cities & Public Sector

For city, county, tribal and state finance leaders: how governments are starting to use USDC and stablecoins, and what to watch in Oklahoma.

An opening for Oklahoma

Colorado, Louisiana and Detroit take crypto, turned into dollars on arrival. No U.S. city we found takes payments in USDC itself. Oklahoma could lead.

Based on our search of public sources as of Oct 22, 2026. A city may have done this without wide coverage.

Sources: Colorado Newsline (Colorado, $57,211 from 78 payments, Sep 2022 to Dec 2024); LA Treasury and GovTech (Louisiana, Sep 2024); City of Detroit release (Nov 2024).

Why going first matters

  • Shape the playbook. The first city writes the procedures other cities are likely to copy.
  • National attention. A first mover tends to draw coverage and interest from peers across the country.
  • Learn from others. Bermuda and Panama City abroad, and Wyoming among the states, have already tried different approaches (see the table below).
  • A federal framework now exists. The GENIUS Act (2025) sets federal rules for payment stablecoin issuers; it takes effect Jan 18, 2027.

What a first city would look at

  • Controls: who can start, review and release a payment, and how records are reconciled.
  • Rules on public funds: what state law allows a city to hold or invest, and for how long.
  • Procurement: how vendors and service providers are chosen under the city's purchasing rules.
  • Open records: how payment records are kept and released under open records law.
  • A bank converting to dollars: as in Panama City, a bank can turn each USDC payment into dollars right away, so the city's books stay in dollars.

The pattern so far

  • Accepting payments, then converting. Panama City's model has a bank turn each stablecoin payment into dollars at once, so the government's books stay in dollars.
  • Paying out. Bermuda's 2026 pilot extends USDC to government payments, not just collections.
  • Issuing their own. Wyoming chose to issue a state token rather than use USDC.
  • USDC is not a bank deposit and is not insured by the FDIC, so rules on public funds and collateral matter before any government holds it.

5. Learn

Basics in plain English for anyone new to stablecoins.

Stablecoins in 60 seconds

  1. A stablecoin is a digital dollar. A company issues one token for each dollar it holds in reserve and promises to pay a dollar back on request (called redemption).
  2. USDC is the second largest. It is issued by Circle, a U.S. public company (NYSE: CRCL). Its reserves sit mainly in a BlackRock government money market fund, held at BNY, plus cash at large banks.
  3. It moves on a blockchain: a shared digital ledger, always on, that records who holds what. Transfers can move any hour, any day, including weekends.
  4. It is not a deposit. USDC is not insured by the FDIC. The GENIUS Act (2025) bars issuers from paying interest, but some exchanges pay "rewards" on it, which is why bankers worry about deposit competition.

Key terms

StablecoinA digital token meant to stay worth one dollar, backed by reserves the issuer holds.
USDCThe U.S. dollar stablecoin issued by Circle.
BlockchainA shared digital ledger, always on, that records who holds what.
RedemptionTurning a stablecoin back into dollars with the issuer.
ReservesThe cash and Treasury holdings with short maturities that back each token.
Deposit tokenA tokenized bank deposit, such as JPMorgan's JPM Coin. It is a deposit, not a stablecoin.
DeFi lendingAutomated lending pools run by software on a blockchain, with no bank in the middle.
GENIUS ActThe 2025 federal law that sets rules for U.S. payment stablecoin issuers.
KeysThe digital passwords that control a blockchain wallet. Whoever holds the keys controls the funds. MA never holds or controls keys.
