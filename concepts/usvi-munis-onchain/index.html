The idea

What Munis records today.
Provable on Solana tomorrow.

The Government of the Virgin Islands runs Tyler Munis as its system for public finance. This concept shows how the same work could publish a tamper evident public record on Solana, with USDC settlement where a bank and the Department of Finance choose it. Munis, or whatever follows it, stays the system of record in this phase.

Sample purchase

Purchasing, then accounts payable

Requisition in GVIBuy (since June 2026), then a purchase order encumbered in Munis, then receipt, invoice, and payment. Each approval would write a memo to Solana as an audit trail. Funds move only through the government's bank.

1. Requisition
The department submits it in GVIBuy.
2. Purchase order approved
Property and Procurement approves. Munis encumbers the funds.
3. Goods received
The receiving report is signed.
4. Invoice matched
A 3 way match in accounts payable.
5. Bank pays
The bank releases USDC or ACH.
6. Receipt anchored
A Solana memo carries the hash.

Limits

Guardrails and assumptions

  • All government funds move through a regulated bank. The bank and the Department of Finance authorize every payment.
  • Majestic Advising and GotSOL.app never hold, control, or recover keys.
  • No personal data on chain. Only hashes, totals, and approval memos.
  • Munis stays the system of record in this phase. On chain anchoring is an addition, not a replacement.
  • The maintenance figures (about $940,000, and about $1.17 million in Department of Finance lines) come from Legislature Post Audit analyses. The signed Tyler contract is not public and is not represented here. There is no contract file on this page.
  • An open records request to the Department of Finance and the Department of Property and Procurement would be the way to see the current Munis agreement. A later phase could then try one fund. This is not the first pilot.
