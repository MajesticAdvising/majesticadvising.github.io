How the money moves

Every payment goes through RT Park's bank

Money comes in, RT Park's bank checks and authorizes it, and it lands in RT Park's treasury as USDC. From there it goes to programs, then out to employees and vendors. The public can watch each step.

1. Money in

  • Tenant company fees
  • Federal grants
  • GVI loan fund money (Act 8464)

2. RT Park's bank

  • Circle signs it on the bank's instruction

3. RT Park treasury

  • Holds USDC
  • Balance is public

4. Programs

  • STEM, business growth, office, UVI support
  • Each has its own wallet

5. Paid out

  • Payroll in USDC
  • Vendors and loans
  • Only to the allowed payee list

Why a public ledger helps

RT Park asks for no General Fund money for FY 2027 and pays its own way. A public ledger lets senators, UVI and residents check that claim at any time, not only at a yearly hearing.

The Legislature's Post Audit Division noted that RT Park's FY 2020 audit found weak controls over payments and payroll, and that RT Park reports it has since fixed them. A public record of every payment is one simple way to show that.

Privacy choice

A public ledger should not post a named person's pay. In this demo every worker is an ID such as Employee 004. RT Park would keep the list that links IDs to people private. The public sees totals, programs and timing, not who earns what.

Vendors and borrowers are shown by number too. The same rule could apply to anyone who asks for it.

Ground rules for this concept

  • Funds always move through a bank. RT Park's bank authorizes every transaction, and Circle signs it on the bank's instruction.
  • Majestic Advising never holds funds or keys.
  • USDC is not a bank deposit and is not FDIC insured.
  • This is a concept demo. RT Park has not agreed to any part of it.
