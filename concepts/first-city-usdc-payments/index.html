Why going first matters

  1. The first city shapes the playbook. Other cities are likely to copy its procedures.
  2. National attention. A first city tends to draw coverage and interest from peers across the country.
  3. Lessons from Bermuda, Panama City and Wyoming. Each tried a different approach.
  4. A federal framework now exists. The GENIUS Act sets federal rules for payment stablecoins, effective Jan 18, 2027.

What a first city would plan for

  1. Controls: who starts, reviews and releases a payment, and how records are reconciled.
  2. Public funds rules: what state law lets a city hold or invest, and for how long.
  3. Procurement: how the city chooses vendors under its purchasing rules.
  4. Open records: how payment records are kept and released.
  5. A bank converting USDC to dollars, so the city's books stay in dollars.

Three lessons

    Ground rules for this concept

    • The city accepts USDC payments, and a bank converts them to dollars.
    • The city's funds always move through a bank.
    • Majestic Advising never moves, holds or converts funds, and never holds or controls keys.
    • USDC is not a bank deposit and is not FDIC insured.
    • This is a concept demo for one Oklahoma municipal government (name held in confidence). Nothing is signed, and no agency has endorsed it.
